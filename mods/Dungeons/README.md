## ✨ Features

Each dungeon has an entrance in the world. Behind it, every player's game builds the same rooms from the dungeon's kind and its seed, high above the entrance, the way the game builds its own crypts. Only the entrance is saved: which dungeon it is, its seed, and how far players got.

Dungeons don't appear in the world by themselves yet. For now, an admin or a tester puts an entrance down with a test command (see the commands below).

### Going in and out

- Press E on the entrance to go in. If the dungeon is still forming, you go in as soon as it's ready.
- Press E on the way out, the door in the first room, to come back out in front of the entrance.
- Once someone has been in, the entrance shows the day the dungeon comes back new.

### The dungeons

- Shifting Halls: a Giants' clockwork vault. Ancient Jotunn machinery in grausten and black marble, bronze and iron gears, chains, and cold blue light. A hall four storeys high with turning staircases driven by gears, levers, a round drum room that turns every 12 seconds, and a vault at the top behind a gate, where the Jotunn's great carved chest holds the reward. Every room in it is new, made for this mod, and most are built fresh for each dungeon: two rooms with the same job (a fight, a lever, a pit to cross) look and play differently.
- On the way: fight rooms that lock until their waves are beaten (with cover and pillars to fight round), quieter rooms with something of their own (a guarded chest, a lever alcove, a hidden stash behind a cracked wall), secret rooms behind walls a pickaxe or a club can break, a shortcut back to the entrance once you've come far enough, and the Clockwork Warden, a boss with its own health bar.
- Its entrance is a giant iron door in a hill. Beside it lie the bones of a fallen giant: a skull, a sword and a huge cogwheel.
- Cathedral of the Bone Choir: a cathedral built inside a giant skeleton. Tall pointed doorways and pale stone, every vault a ribcage, pillars of bound bones, skeleton singers on stone thrones, and only candles and firebowls for light.
- In the Bone Choir: ring the bell in the Nave of Ribs and the dead come in four waves while stone spikes drop on the marked lanes (they shudder first). In the Bell Tower the bells ring a hymn by themselves: pull their ropes in the same order and the lift rises. In the Charnel, push the bone cart round the skull piles onto the open grave. Three relics, laid on their carved pedestals in the Reliquary, open the way on, and a checkpoint waits there.
- Its bosses: the Choirmaster, who can't be hurt while a choir stall sings (drop that stall's shutter with the lever beside it), and the Bone Colossus, whom the choir sings back to its mound behind a shield (pull the four bell ropes in the order the bells ring).
- The Drowned Vault: a sea temple cut into a sea cliff, sunk long ago and drained. Dark banded sea-rock, floors of weathered ship's decks, wrecked longships rammed through the walls, ceilings ribbed like an upturned hull, kelp hanging from the vaults, and green sea-fire for light. Its entrance is a way into a sea cliff half-sunk in sand, with an iron anchor leaning on it.
- In the Drowned Vault: in the Tide Locks, sluice levers raise and lower the tide under three drowned hulls. Low, a hull's deck is the path through the arcades under the piers; high, it's level with the pier tops. Raising one path drowns another, and the emblems by each lever match the hulls it moves. In the Anchor Gallery, four iron anchors swing across the hall in turn (each creaks and shudders first). In the Kelp Way, timber tide-gates slam down in turn after a warning. Opening the drowned crew's sea chest in the Wreck Hall wakes them.
- Its bosses: the Bellkeeper in its bell tower (lure it under the great bell and pull a bell-rope: the bell drops on it), and Jarl Hafgrim the Drowned in his mead-hall, who calls the tide: the side aisles flood with a freezing surge every few seconds while he sits shielded on his throne (shut both sluice gates together, twice).
- Rimeforge Depths: a frost-giants' smithy that a glacier swallowed, its forges still smouldering inside the ice. Tall forge halls of black stone with glacier ice grown over them, black iron ribs and beams, and orange forge light against the ice's pale blue. Its entrance is a smoking crack in a glacier wall, with a giant's hammer lying head-down in the ice beside it.
- In Rimeforge: walls of ice close every way on until all three Frozen Forges burn at once (pull each furnace's bellows lever, or light it with a torch; each burns only 14 seconds, and steam vents scald across the hall on a beat). In the Hammer Line four trip-hammers slam across the hall in turn (each creaks and smokes first), until a lever at the far end stops them. A secret strongroom hides behind cracked ice.
- Its bosses: the Bellows-Keeper, whose frost-crusted hide turns most blows until you lure it into a bellows lane and pull that bellows' lever, and the Rimesmith, whose rime armour breaks under a falling drop-hammer press, and who turns white-hot and shielded until you push blocks of glacier ice into the forge's mouth, twice.
- Rootdeep Hollow: down among the roots of a dying world-tree. Floors of packed earth, walls of dark layered rock with pale roots woven over them, great roots arching across every roof, and the cold glow of mushrooms and guck pods for light; amber marks anything you can use. Its entrance is a great root arching out of the ground, split open at its foot.
- In Rootdeep: the Sap Shaft is a hollow trunk three storeys tall, where each sap valve makes two root bridges grow up level with the ledges or wither into the dark; work out the order to climb to the heart's door. In the Seed Garden a sap valve drops a glowing seed from a pod; put it in the root socket at the rot pit's edge and a root bridge grows across (the seed belongs to the dungeon and vanishes if taken outside).
- Its bosses: the Sap-Warden, which goes back to drink from the Mother Root and can't be hurt until you carry a fallen seed to the root's mouth, and the Rotheart, which hides behind a rot-shield while the chamber's root walls close in, until all three rot-nodes burn at once (pull the amber sap-fire bloom beside each, or use a torch or fire arrows). Its spores are poison.
- The Starfall Orrery: a sky-watchers' observatory built into the crater where a star fell. Tall round halls of pale stone banded in black marble, star-stones hung on chains from night-black vaults, silver armillary hoops tumbling overhead, and cold white starlight. Its entrance is a ring of pale standing stones round a glowing meteorite, with a moon-door: a dark stone disc in a silver ring.
- In the Starfall: in the Great Orrery, three geared star-cranks each turn one, two or three planets round the fallen star; bring all three into line with the far door (a planet lights up when it's in line) and the moon-bridge rises across the star-well. In the Star-Chart Halls the floor is a star map: step on the constellation's stars in order (a wrong step starts it over), then beat the watchers' shades it raises. Stars fall from the vault onto pale rings on a beat, with a whistle first.
- Its bosses: the Astrolabe Keeper, whose star-iron hide turns most blows until you lure it onto the pale ring as the sighting-lens strikes, and the Sun-Eater, who hides on its dais when the black moon covers the sun, until you turn all three sun-mirrors to throw their light onto it at once, twice.
- Test Crypt: the game's own Black Forest crypt rooms, joined in new ways. Made to test the dungeon system.
- Test Mechanisms: a small dungeon with one of each moving part: a lever, a pressure plate, a turning stair, a lift and a gate.
- Test Blocks: a small dungeon with the other building blocks: a pedestal for a key, braziers to light, a block to push, a trap, teleporters, a locked gate and a checkpoint.
- The same seed always gives the same dungeon. A dungeon's puzzle is checked when it's laid out, so it can always be solved.

### Moving parts and puzzles

- Levers: press E to pull one. A lever can turn a staircase, open a gate or start a machine.
- Pressure plates: stand on one to press it.
- Turning staircases turn a quarter at a time and join a different pair of landings. Lifts carry you up and down. Some rooms turn by themselves, on a timer.
- Some machinery just turns: gears that spin while their mechanism moves, and backwards when it goes back.
- Anything that moves carries whoever stands on it, up, down and round.
- Every player sees every moving part in the same place at the same time.
- In the Shifting Halls, set the stairs in the right order to reach the vault. It takes about 12 to 17 uses of the levers and plates.

### Boss fights

- A dungeon ends in a boss fight. The doors shut, the boss arrives with a banner, and the room changes as the fight goes on.
- Its big attacks are shown first: a red ring on the floor (or a lane for a charge) and a sound. Roll out of the way.
- Bosses fight in phases. Some put up a shield that only the room's machinery breaks: light the braziers, or lure the boss under a hammer and pull its lever. Some get a second wind: at low health they recover part of it and come back faster and harder-hitting.
- The messages say plainly what's happening and what to do.
- One player can win every fight. Each extra player inside makes a boss tougher.
- In the Shifting Halls: a mini boss, the Cog-Thrall, on the way, and the Clockwork Warden at the end. The Warden leaves a trophy for an item stand.

### Enemies and loot that fit the land

- Enemies fit the land the entrance stands in. A dungeon in the Mountains has Mountain enemies (and some from the Swamp), and nothing from a land further on. A creature that doesn't fit is swapped for one of the same kind (a fighter, an archer or a flyer) that does.
- None of the game's bosses (the Clockwork Warden is the land's toughest enemy, made stronger). Enemies have up to 1 star in the Meadows and the Black Forest, and up to 2 stars from the Swamp on.
- Loot fits the land too. Chests are filled with things from that land (or an earlier one), and the vault's chests hold more. Nothing inside gives anything from a land further on: plants to pick, pots, rocks and the pieces you can break keep only what fits.
- A room's enemies appear once, the first time someone comes in.
- A dungeon's walls, doors, chests and furniture can't be broken or taken down with the hammer. Pots, bone piles, roots and the cracked walls of secret rooms still break.

### Coming back new

- Some in-game days after a dungeon was first entered (3, set by the server), it comes back new: its enemies, its loot and its puzzle.
- It never happens while anyone is inside.
- Anything you left in one of its chests is put on the ground in front of the entrance, so you don't lose it. Creatures still alive and items on the floor stay.

### Safe inside

- You can't fall out of a dungeon. If you get there before it has formed (after a teleport, or a lag spike), you are held still until it has.
- Log out inside and you log back in at the entrance, even if the mod was removed in between.
- Die inside and your gravestone stays inside, where you died, as in the game's own crypts.

### Commands for admins and testers

- With our Claude Tools mod, type them in the game's console (F5) after "claude". For example, claude dungeon spawn shiftinghalls puts a Shifting Halls entrance in front of you (the others: bonechoir, drowned, rimeforge, rootdeep, starfall, testcrypt, testmechs, testblocks).
- dungeon list shows the dungeons in the world, dungeon map a dungeon's layout floor by floor, dungeon solve its puzzle's solution, dungeon reset makes it new again now, and dungeon remove takes it away for good.
- Commands that change the world follow the game's cheat rule: they work in single player, for the host, or with devcommands on.

### Your own dungeons

- A kind of dungeon is a pack: one file of data with its rooms, moving parts, enemies, loot and entrance. New kinds need no code.
- Put packs in BepInEx/config/Dungeons/packs (and entrance looks in packs/looks in it). They are read when a world starts.
- On a server, only the server's packs count. It sends them to each player as they join, so players install nothing.

### Coming

- Dungeons placed in the world for you to find as you explore, with entrances that fit the land.

Everyone needs it, and the host above all: each dungeon's entrance is saved in the world, with the dungeon's state, and a game without the mod deletes the entrances when their area loads. The server and every player need the same version of the mod: a player with another version, or without it, can't join.

## 👥 Multiplayer

**Who needs it:** Only the players who want it.

It adds dungeon entrances (objects the world saves). Restart the game after installing or updating it so they register cleanly: an entrance whose area loads while it is not registered is deleted by the host. The host and every player need the same version.

<!-- HAND-WRITTEN: multiplayer -->
**On a dedicated server, the server needs the mod too**, or it deletes the entrances. The rooms themselves are built on each player's own game, from the dungeon's seed, so they are the same for everyone. Moving parts follow the server's clock: every player sees a staircase turn at the same moment.

**The host decides** how many in-game days pass before a dungeon comes back new. While you play on their world their value replaces yours (your file isn't changed).

## 🌍 Languages

**English, Русский (Russian), Deutsch (German), 简体中文 (Chinese), Português do Brasil (Portuguese), Español (Spanish), Français (French), Polski (Polish), Türkçe (Turkish).** It follows the game's own language setting.

The translations were made with AI, so a few words may be off. To correct one, or to add your language, put a file in `BepInEx/config/translations/com.quad.dungeons/` named after the game's name for the language (`German.json`, `Italian.json`, `Japanese.json`...) with only the lines you change, copied from [English.json](https://github.com/HardHeadHackerHead/valheim-mods/blob/main/mods/Dungeons/translations/English.json) (it wins over the built-in one). Or [tell us](https://github.com/HardHeadHackerHead/valheim-mods/issues/new?template=bug.yml&title=%5BQuad%27s%20Dungeons%5D%20Translation%3A%20) the better words and we'll put them in for everyone.

## ⚙️ Settings

In `BepInEx/config/com.quad.dungeons.cfg`, made the first time the game runs with the mod. r2modman and Thunderstore Mod Manager edit it under **Config editor**.<!-- GITHUB-ONLY --> With our mod manager: **F7**, then **Mod settings**.<!-- END GITHUB-ONLY -->

**1 - Dungeons**

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `ResetDays` | `3` | In-game days after a dungeon is first entered before it comes back new: its enemies, loot and puzzle (only once nobody is inside). 0: never. [Synced with Server] |

**2 - Pack making**

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `ReloadPacksOnSave` | `false` | Reload BepInEx/config/Dungeons/packs a second after a file there is saved (as `dungeon reload`), in single player or as the host. Dungeons of a changed pack are remade, unless someone is inside. For people making packs; off for play. |

**3 - Look and feel**

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `ScreenShake` | `50` | How much the camera shakes at a dungeon boss's big moments (its landing, a shield breaking, its death), in percent. 0: never. Your own, not synced. |

## 📜 Recent changes

- **0.4.0** Five new dungeons, each its own place with its own puzzles, traps and two boss fights: the Drowned Vault (a sunken sea temple: raise and lower drowned hulls in the Tide Locks; the Bellkeeper and Jarl Hafgrim), the Cathedral of the Bone Choir (ring the bells in the hymn's order, gather three relics; the Choirmaster and the Bone Colossus), Rimeforge Depths (a frost-giants' smithy in a glacier: light three frozen forges at once, run the Hammer Line; the Bellows-Keeper and the Rimesmith), Rootdeep Hollow (the roots of a dying world-tree: sap valves grow and wither root bridges, plant a seed to cross a rot pit; the Sap-Warden and the Rotheart) and the Starfall Orrery (an observatory in a star's crater: line up the planets to raise the moon-bridge, walk a constellation; the Astrolabe Keeper and the Sun-Eater, beaten by aiming sun-mirrors). Much smoother: a dungeon nobody is in now costs almost nothing (before, every dungeon standing in the world cost frame time even when empty: 60 FPS with six dungeons now, about 10 before); rooms you can't see stop their fires, smoke and effects too. Fight rooms and plate rooms often have a second way out. Shifting Halls and the Dvergr test pack are rebuilt with these rooms. For pack makers: light beams on any mechanism (beams), dungeon solve says what to do and where for every step, dungeon perf shows where each frame goes, the gate checks every part stays inside its room, and theme hooks for trap looks and spec [items]/[pack] tables.
- **0.3.3** Smoother inside dungeons: a dungeon you aren't in draws nothing, and inside one only the rooms near you are drawn (rooms at a teleport's landing are drawn before you arrive; boss warnings are never hidden). The corridor hammers are real traps now: a chain creaks and the block lifts before it slams to the floor, with damage fitted to the land. Stash chests and secret rooms are marked in the pack so the map hides them until their wall breaks. Fixed: a warning flooding the log every frame after a dungeon was removed (leftovers were held with a method Unity complains about; nothing is left held any more). For testers: dungeon perf (frame rate, what's drawn, culling on/off).
- **0.3.2** Chests hidden behind cracked walls (stashes and secret rooms) no longer show on the map before the wall is broken. "The doors slam shut!" only shows when doors really shut; small fights say "Enemies close in!" or "The chest's guardians wake!". dungeon tour says which chests and doors weren't loaded for its pictures.
